# Storyboard Rules

A storyboard is one continuous piece of footage. It is not a grid of unrelated
Chihuahua ideas.

## Before storyboarding

Require an approved first frame or an explicit user instruction to bypass the
gate. Carry forward:

- Chihuahua face, eyes, body proportions, durag, clothing, and scale
- props and vehicles
- room, road, building, doors, furniture, and landmarks
- time of day, weather, lighting, palette, and shadows
- console build, texture density, and camera grammar
- subject facing, travel direction, and current action state

## Choose the smallest shot count

- MINI: 1-3 shots
- SCENE: 4-6 shots
- FAKE AD: 4-6 shots
- BUMPER: no storyboard by default

Use fewer shots when they make the action clearer.

## Build progressive action

Use a structure like:

1. establish or hook
2. discovery or first action
3. movement or proof
4. escalation or perspective shift
5. reaction or decision
6. payoff, reveal, cliffhanger, or loop condition

Every shot must imply what just happened, what is happening now, and what the
next cut reveals. Do not replay the same beat from six angles.

Track state between shots:

- what Chihuahua holds and in which paw
- which object is in front, behind, left, or right
- direction of travel and facing
- which door or vehicle is used
- distance between characters
- damage, open/closed state, and completed actions

If Chihuahua picks up a phone, the next shot must keep it in his paw unless the
action shows him putting it down.

## Vary camera with purpose

Create rhythm from a deliberate mix of:

- wide environmental establish
- extreme close-up
- low pavement-level view
- high overhead
- profile
- rear-follow
- medium tracking view
- foreground obstruction
- tight reaction
- low-angle reveal
- selective dutch angle

Avoid six centered eye-level medium shots. Avoid random extremes that hide the
action or break geography.

Use old-game camera behavior: fixed cuts, short pans, basic tracking, crude
dolly movement, restrained orbiting, and practical push-ins.

## Contact-sheet generation

When generating one board:

- use equal-size panels with thin dark gutters
- use 2x2 for four shots, 3x2 for six shots, or another clear reading grid
- read left-to-right, top-to-bottom
- keep one rendering contract in every panel
- preserve identical Chihuahua, clothing, props, vehicle, and environment assets
- preserve screen direction and progressive action
- omit captions, numbers, HUD, logos, and interface text unless requested
- never add extra limbs or another Chihuahua to explain motion

If a video model repeatedly treats a contact sheet as a collage, animate shots
separately from the same project lock and assemble them in editing.

## Storyboard delivery

When direct image generation is available, generate the clean board. Otherwise
return the complete production prompt.

End with:

> If the sequence is right, reply **STORYBOARD APPROVED**. If not, name the shot
> and the one thing that should change.

For a requested shot repair, lock every other panel and change only that shot.
