## Purpose and activation

Adapter ID: `late-90s-ova-crime-action-v1`

Adapter version: `1.1`

Display signifier: `LATE-90s OVA CRIME/ACTION`

Use for Chihuahua images and cinematics that request late-1990s OVA,
Yu Yu Hakusho-leaning broadcast cel, analog-anime noir, adult crime anime,
grounded cel suspense, or the bundled approved sheet. The character drawing
combines early-to-mid-1990s supernatural-action television economy with
late-1990s crime/OVA restraint. This adapter is for patient tension, urban
atmosphere, procedural behavior, dangerous meetings, surveillance, pursuit,
and short physical confrontations. It replaces the default PS2 build while
active. Do not mix cel animation and 3D game rendering unless the user requests
a hybrid.

Keep `LATE-Z BATTLE CEL` distinct: route superhuman combat, power-ups,
transformations, extended aerial fighting, and tournament-style spectacle to
Late-Z unless the user explicitly selects OVA treatment. OVA action remains
physical, economical, spatially grounded, and consequential.

The adapter borrows period production grammar only. Do not add franchise
characters, costumes, symbols, locations, logos, or story canon unless the
user separately requests them.

Ground visual research in ordinary in-episode frames from original 4:3 SD
broadcast, VHS, LaserDisc, or period DVD sources. Exclude Blu-ray restorations,
AI upscales, remastered color, covers, posters, promotional stills, production
cels photographed outside their final scene, fan art, and contemporary retro
illustrations from style authority.

## Reference-role firewall

Assign every supplied reference one primary role before generation:

- **IDENTITY:** canonical Chihuahua sheet or approved project Chihuahua
- **STYLE:** bundled OVA sheet and verified in-episode original-SD cel frames
- **PROJECT:** approved frame or storyboard controlling current continuity
- **MOTION:** clips controlling only timing, cuts, camera rhythm, blocking,
  pose cadence, or effects behavior

A motion reference must not import its character identity, clothing, anatomy,
palette, location, crop, subtitles, watermark, or audio. A modern anime clip may
inform one named movement but must not modernize the selected OVA finish. Never
use remastered or promotional imagery as silent style authority.

## Rendering and palette lock

- original 4:3 standard-definition photographed-cel presentation, never
  remastered sharpness or modern promotional polish
- character construction leaning toward 1992-1995 supernatural-action TV cels:
  direct silhouettes, firm brow-to-muzzle planes, serious readable poses, and
  economical drawing rather than cute mascot treatment
- confident dark brown-black ink, moderately heavy outer silhouettes, slightly
  softer photographed edges, and very few purposeful interior marks
- simplified forms built from one flat base, one hard-edged shadow color, and
  only an occasional tiny highlight; no soft character gradients
- warm muted ochre and tobacco-tan fur with a single brown-gray shadow tone;
  indicate fur with clean silhouette and only a few short graphic notches at
  cheeks, chest edge, elbows, or thighs
- near-solid dark brown-black eyes with exactly one small hard white catchlight
  per eye; no radial iris, layered reflection, wet-glass rendering, or visible
  human sclera
- dusty-rose durag with one burgundy shadow shape; preserve forehead band, rear
  knot, exactly two tails, and broad graphic folds without fabric texture
- subdued charcoal, dirty teal, steel blue, nicotine cream, faded mauve, sodium
  amber, and deep green-black environment colors
- hand-painted backgrounds with broad shapes, selective architectural detail,
  atmospheric recession, and shadow masses rather than digital texture density
- restrained original-SD softness, mild analog gamma, minute paint registration
  imperfection, and slight chroma bleed at color boundaries
- darkness retains readable shape and local color; avoid crushed silhouettes
  that erase the Chihuahua or his anatomy

The result should resemble an ordinary frame from an original episode master,
not an intentionally distressed VHS simulation. Any grain or softness is a
subordinate finishing characteristic and must remain stable during animation.

## Chihuahua translation

Keep the exact tiny tan Chihuahua, upright and bipedal, with compact torso,
short limbs, paw-like hands, large ears, short muzzle, tiny nose, oversized dark
eyes, and complete dusty-pink durag. Preserve canine facial structure and very
small scale relative to doors, cars, furniture, and adult supporting characters.

The eyes retain canonical scale and spacing while their upper edges may become
firmer and slightly angular, as shown in the approved sheet. Keep the fill
nearly solid and the highlight singular. Use the presets below only when the
story needs understated OVA acting. They alter visible eyelid aperture, brow
tension, head angle, eye direction, and mouth set—not eye size, placement,
dark canine fill, muzzle, ears, or head construction.

## Expression presets

### NOIR_GUARDED

- firm upper-lid line over the same oversized near-solid dark eyes
- exactly one small hard highlight retained in each eye
- relaxed lower lids, level brow, closed mouth, and a controlled side-glance
- calm suspicion rather than anger, sleepiness, cuteness, or a redesign

### THREAT_ALERT

- upper lids lift slightly while the lower lids and brow-to-muzzle plane tighten
- near-solid dark canine fill remains forward and readable
- exactly one highlight per eye, one short brow crease, ears upright, mouth
  closed, and jaw subtly set
- controlled determination rather than a wide cartoon shock expression

Never add large white sclera, bright irises, layered glossy eyes, multiple
catchlights, human eye anatomy, human teeth, or rubbery facial deformation.

## World and lighting grammar

Favor plausible late-night or transitional spaces: closed storefronts, alleys,
parking structures, train platforms, motel corridors, offices after hours,
industrial edges, apartment interiors, diners, docks, and quiet roadways.
Daylight is allowed but should retain subdued practical color and purposeful
shadow design.

Light comes from visible or credible sources: fluorescent tubes, vending
machines, desk lamps, televisions, streetlights, headlights, window light,
clouded sky, or hard afternoon sun. Use reflected wet-street color sparingly.
Keep colored light localized; do not turn ordinary city scenes into neon-heavy
cyberpunk.

## Camera and composition

Favor adult-thriller framing:

- low medium-wides that make the Chihuahua feel small inside the environment
- guarded medium close-ups and eye-line cuts for suspicion or recognition
- long-lens observation frames through doorways, windows, fencing, or vehicles
- high-angle geography shots before a dangerous approach
- inserts of one meaningful prop, reflection, paw, eye, lock, phone, or evidence
- wide aftermath frames that hold negative space after the action ends
- strong asymmetry, foreground occlusion, practical leading lines, and clear
  screen direction

Use locked compositions, restrained pans, short tracks, small optical push-ins,
and decisive hard cuts. Reserve handheld instability for one brief moment of
physical disruption. Avoid constant camera movement, orbiting, drone grammar,
extreme wide-angle distortion, glossy depth of field, or arbitrary dutch angles.

Keep 4:3 unless the user explicitly requests another format.

## Temporal rhythm

For MINI, SCENE, BUMPER, or FAKE AD animation briefs:

- build tension through holds, eyelines, off-screen sound implications, and
  environmental motion before one decisive event
- let principal shots breathe; inserts are brief and motivated
- tag every shot `HOLD`, `BURST`, `INSERT`, or `REVEAL`
- give each shot one dominant motion channel: subject, camera, or environment
- favor held drawings with small eye, ear, head, paw, rain, smoke, fluorescent,
  traffic-light, or durag-tail changes
- use stepped pose changes and economical cel replacement rather than perfectly
  smooth modern interpolation
- use hard cuts by default; allow one short match cut, reflection cut, or slow
  dissolve only when it serves location or time passage
- during action, show anticipation, one readable physical path, contact or
  evasion, recovery, and a held consequence
- permit a single brief smear, dry-brush speed accent, or hard impact cel when
  physical action needs emphasis; never sustain a battle-anime effects field
- preserve contour, face, cel-shadow geometry, palette, and grain through every
  shot; no line boil, facial redrawing, or elastic zooming

Sound may be implied visually, but follow the project's declared audio intent.
`NO MUSIC` permits practical ambience and effects; `NO AUDIO` permits neither.

## Motion profiles

### SUSPENSE_BUILD

Use for surveillance, dangerous meetings, discoveries, stalking, waiting, and
pre-confrontation scenes:

1. establish geography and one environmental rhythm
2. hold on Chihuahua observing or waiting
3. insert one meaningful cue or off-screen disturbance
4. show a restrained recognition or decision
5. reveal the threat, absence, object, or changed situation
6. hold the final implication instead of over-explaining it

Keep subject movement small until the decision. Camera movement should clarify
information, not manufacture energy.

### GROUNDED_ACTION

Use for a short chase, escape, takedown, struggle, collision, or single exchange:

1. establish positions and escape path
2. show one committed movement
3. use one brief contact, evasion, or obstruction insert
4. show recovery or changed geography
5. hold the consequence or threatening aftermath

Keep weight, obstacles, distance, and direction legible. Use one action path per
principal shot. Chihuahua may be unusually capable, but he still interacts with
the physical environment; do not convert the sequence into flight, aura combat,
teleportation, or prolonged superhuman melee. Preserve one head, one torso, two
forearms, and two hind legs through contact.

For 8-15 seconds, prefer 4-5 principal shots plus no more than two extremely
brief inserts. Written durations are rhythm guidance, not guaranteed frame-level
control.

## Repair checks

Repair only the failed layer:

- **too clean or digital:** add very light cel-photography grain, mild broadcast
  softness, and minute paint registration imperfection; preserve composition
- **looks remastered or promotional:** reduce edge acuity, highlight count,
  surface detail, color intensity, and theatrical lighting; restore ordinary
  in-episode framing and restrained original-SD color
- **too dark:** lift local cel values around Chihuahua and preserve hard shadow
  design; do not add bloom or global gray haze
- **too painterly:** remove dense fur strokes, hatching, texture, and soft blends;
  restore bold economical ink, broad opaque paint planes, and one hard shadow
  tone
- **too Late-Z:** remove aura, speed-line fields, tournament staging, extreme
  battle expressions, and weightless motion; restore grounded geography and
  restrained acting
- **too cyberpunk:** limit colored light to credible sources; restore dirty teal,
  charcoal, sodium amber, tobacco tan, and faded mauve
- **Chihuahua feels too large:** restore small scale against environmental anchors
  while preserving camera and composition
- **eyes look modern or human:** remove iris rings, gradients, layered
  reflections, extra catchlights, bright irises, and large white sclera; restore
  oversized near-solid dark canine fill, canonical spacing, and exactly one
  small hard highlight per eye
- **guarded expression looks sleepy:** lift the gaze and ear posture while
  retaining partially lowered upper lids
- **alert expression becomes cartoon shock:** close the mouth, reduce lid lift,
  and use one brow crease with a controlled head turn
- **identity drifts:** restore canonical breed, face, bipedal proportions, short
  limbs, muzzle, ears, tan fur, small scale, and complete dusty-pink durag
- **fake aging becomes obvious:** reduce grain and remove VHS artifacts, borders,
  scratches, scanlines, and color casts
- **camera feels inert:** strengthen eyeline, foreground occlusion, negative
  space, or one restrained push-in; do not add constant motion
- **action becomes weightless or creates limbs:** reduce it to one physical path,
  restore exact anatomy, and re-establish floor contact, obstacle, and consequence
- **held drawing crawls:** stabilize contour, face, cel shadows, and grain;
  animate only the declared dominant motion channel
