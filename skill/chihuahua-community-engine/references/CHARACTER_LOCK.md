Use the bundled character sheet as the default and canonical visual authority:

`references/chihuahua-character-sheet.png`

Apply this lock automatically for IMAGE, MINI, SCENE, BUMPER, and FAKE AD. The
user does not need to upload or redescribe the Chihuahua after installing the
Skill. When image-generation tooling supports image references, supply this
sheet as the identity reference whenever practical. If text and the sheet
conflict, the sheet controls character identity unless the user explicitly
requests a change.

Assign it to identity, face, proportions, fur color, eye scale, breed, ear
shape, muzzle, body DNA, and default durag. Rebuild clothing, props, and the
world around this identity.

## Locked identity

- small tan to light-brown Chihuahua
- upright and bipedal by default
- compact pear-shaped torso
- short hind legs and small flat paws
- short forearms with paw-like hands
- large Chihuahua ears with simple triangular construction
- short narrow muzzle and tiny black nose
- oversized round dark glossy eyes
- slightly serious, blank, worried, or stern resting expression
- faded pink or dusty-rose durag
- durag band across the forehead, knot behind the head, and hanging fabric tails
- intentionally simple early-3D game-character construction

Preserve the eye spacing, brow area, muzzle width, nose scale, head-to-body
ratio, short limbs, and very small overall scale. These make the character
recognizable.

## Bipedal rule

Treat bipedal anatomy as a hard default, not a suggestion.

For standing, sitting, driving, working, running, swimming, climbing, or using
objects:

- keep the torso vertical unless the action requires leaning
- keep two hind legs below the torso
- keep two small forearms attached at the shoulders
- use paw-like hands rather than human hands
- use a stable bipedal hip and shoulder relationship
- animate with slightly stiff early-game movement

Do not interpret the character as an ordinary dog on four legs unless the user
explicitly asks for a transformation or gag.

## Default prompt core

Use compact wording equivalent to:

```text
The supplied canonical character sheet is the immutable identity authority: the same
tiny tan Chihuahua, upright and bipedal, compact torso, short legs, small
forearms with paw-like hands, oversized dark glossy eyes, upright ears, short
muzzle, tiny black nose, and faded dusty-pink durag tied behind the head with
hanging tails. Preserve his exact breed, face, eye scale, head-to-body ratio,
small stature, and simple early-3D construction.
```

## Flexible layers

Allow the user to change:

- outfit and uniform
- accessories
- occupation or social role
- mood
- location and time of day
- vehicle and props
- supporting cast
- genre and situation

Fit new clothing to the compact body. Keep it slightly blocky and compatible
with the selected game build. Oversized clothing may be baggy, but it must not
turn the body into an adult human or bodybuilder.

## Expressions

Use simple readable states:

- stern
- surprised
- suspicious
- tired
- worried
- blank
- quietly pleased

Favor head angle, posture, eye direction, and a small mouth change. Avoid
rubbery modern facial animation, giant smiles, human teeth, or expressive
Pixar-like deformation.

## Never default to

- quadrupedal dog anatomy
- a human adult body with a Chihuahua head
- human-sized proportions
- bodybuilder anatomy
- realistic human hands or five detailed fingers
- another dog breed
- tiny realistic eyes
- long muzzle
- photoreal fur strands
- modern mascot or animated-film styling
- smooth modern anatomy
- a new durag color when none was requested
- a Chihuahua without the pink durag unless explicitly requested
- missing knot or missing tails
- added collars, logos, chains, or token symbols unless requested

## Reference conflicts

Apply this order:

1. explicit user change
2. latest approved project image
3. supplied clothing, prop, or pose reference within that role
4. canonical character sheet

A clothing reference may change clothing. It may not silently replace the face,
species, proportions, eyes, durag, or scale. A screenshot reference may control
rendering and camera. It may not replace Chihuahua with a game protagonist.

## Continuity lock

After the first image is approved, preserve:

- exact face and head construction
- eye size and placement
- body scale and proportions
- fur tone
- durag color, band, knot, and tails
- current clothing and accessories
- prop side and orientation
- action state

Change only what the user names.
